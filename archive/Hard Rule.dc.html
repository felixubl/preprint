Paper, ink, and one thing you may not have

Six slots, each with one job. No mood colours, no tints of tints. The acid at the end is not a slot — it is the ink of exception, and section 11 rations it.

Ink is used at full strength or not at all

No 60%-opacity headings, no grey that is really ink pretending to be quiet. If something should be quieter, it gets the muted slot — a colour someone chose on purpose.

Signal is for consequence, not for brand

Vermilion appears on destructive confirmation and on nothing else. A system that paints its logo in the warning colour has no warning colour.

The acid is rationed

Citron may only appear on an element that is currently breaking a law. If you see it twice on one screen, one of the two is a bug.

06

Icons: three angles, no curves

The whole set is drawn on a 16-unit grid with a 1.75px stroke, using horizontal, vertical and 45° lines only. No arcs, no circles, no curves — a search glass is a square, a status dot is a square, and the family holds together without anyone policing it.

Pointers

Seven drawn cursors, same three angles as the icons, each one ink filled with a paper keyline so it survives on any surface. Hotspots are declared and locked — a skin may repaint a pointer, never move its point. Hover a tile to take it for a walk.

Left to the operating system

Four states are never drawn, because a custom version only makes a familiar meaning slower to read.

The pointer is a promise

Every cursor here states what will happen if you press. point means something responds. cross means precision is expected of you. grab means the surface moves, not the content.

Dropped entirely on touch, where a drawn pointer is a lie about the input device.

08

Controls

Identical geometry in every skin: 44px standard, 36px compact, 52px prominent. Square-shouldered, 2px on the one that matters, and a pressed state that actually moves. Shown on ink because a control family that only works on paper isn't a family.

hierarchy

Primary takes 2px. Secondary takes 1px. Quiet takes none. Border weight is the hierarchy — no fill juggling required.

size & shape

Radius rises with size — 2 / 3 / 4 — so a large button never looks softer than a small one.

Two exceptions in one view cancel each other

Not discouraged — cancelled. A screen needing two breaks has a hierarchy problem, and neither break is doing the work you think it is.

An exception used twice is an undeclared token

The second time a violation appears, it stops being one. Name it, give it a slot, write its states — or stop using it.

The law must survive the breach

If breaking a rule makes the rule look arbitrary, the rule was wrong. Delete it and stop calling the result an exception.

why it matters

Withdraw every licence and the screen is not worse — it is flatter, and nothing tells you which of the two actions you cannot take back. That is what the budget buys.
