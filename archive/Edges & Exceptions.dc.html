Twelve skins, one skeleton — and every one of them has soft corners, because roundness was filed under contract instead of character. Two changes: give the corner back to the skin, and write down when the system is allowed to disobey itself.

This page is set in the geometry it proposes: square corners, weighted rules, one licensed violation. Nothing here uses a radius it hasn't earned.

01

The diagnosis: it is round because you locked the corner.

Section 04 of the framework prints four radius steps in :root and annotates them “Locked. The same four steps in every skin.” That single line is doing more damage than any colour decision in the file.

A corner is not geometry the way a 44px target is geometry. A target is ergonomics — it belongs to the contract. A corner is tone of voice. Locking it means the drawing office and the arcade cabinet and the monastery all speak with the same softness, and twelve skins collapse into twelve colourways.

02

The corner becomes a skin decision.

Radius leaves :root and joins the five things a skin already decides. The contract keeps only the ratio: four steps, each roughly 1.5× the last, and step one is where the skin's whole character sits. Square is now sayable.

hard0 · 0 · 1 · 2

Blueprint. Monastic Terminal. Night Transit. Anything that claims to be a document or a machine.

cut2 · 3 · 4 · 6

Museum Archive, Luxury Catalogue, Neo-Retro. Paper that has been trimmed, not moulded. This page.

soft7 · 11 · 16 · 24

Soft Laboratory, Sticker Press, Arcade Cabinet. The old default — now a choice three skins actively make.

pillretired

No longer a step. Available to badges and tags only — the two places where a shape genuinely means “this is a label, not a control”.

A skin declares one of the three scales and may not mix them. The contract still holds: four steps, one ratio, same names, same slots.

04

Corners were only half of it.

The other half is that nothing in the file is ever loud. Body copy sits between .78 and .95rem, headings stop at 2.3rem, and every specimen lives inside the same 255px card. Edge is contrast, and there is none — so here is the rest of the prescription.

weight, not colour

Three rule weights instead of one: hairline for internal splits, 1px ink for containers, 2px ink for structure. A single border weight is why every card reads as equally important.

a real scale jump

Body up to 17px, section heads to 3.2rem, one display size past 6rem. The step between levels has to be visible from across the room or hierarchy is theoretical.

stop carding everything

Ten sections of auto-fit minmax(255px) is one layout wearing ten hats. Let some content be a table. Let some be a single wide column. Let one thing be full-bleed.

ink the paper

One inverted band per page. Not decoration — it is the only way a light skin ever gets to show what full-strength ink looks like.

Sterile is what a system looks like when every part of it is equally polite.

05

Exceptions.

You already had this instinct and buried it: .ds-btn--shout is described as “the rule-breaker… once per view”, and it is the only sanctioned violation in the entire framework — one 44px button, explained in a paragraph rather than felt.

Promote it. A violation is only interesting if the rule is real, the licence is written down, and the budget is enforced. Seven exceptions, each with a price.

Two exceptions in one view cancel each other.

Not “are discouraged”. Cancel. If a screen needs a scale break and a colour break, it has a hierarchy problem, and neither break is doing the work you think.

An exception you use twice is a token you haven't declared yet.

The moment a violation appears on a second screen, it stops being a violation. Name it, give it a slot, write its states — or stop using it.

Two edits to the contract: radius moves to the skin, and section 11 gets written. Everything else in the framework survives untouched — which is the point. A system with a licence to disobey needs the rest of its law to be strict.
