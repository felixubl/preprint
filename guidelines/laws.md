# The laws, and their licensed breaches

Seven laws. Each has exactly one licensed breach, a condition that earns it, and
a budget. A deviation that is named, budgeted and marked is a **breach**; an
unnamed one is **drift**.

| # | law | licensed breach | what earns it | budget |
|---|-----|-----------------|---------------|--------|
| 01 | Corners follow the surface's scale. | One element borrows a radius from another scale. | When a shape must read as a thing you touch, not a region you are inside. | 1 / view |
| 02 | Type sits on the scale. | One line leaves it — size, weight or face. | When that line is the reason the page exists. Not a heading: the content. | 1 / page |
| 03 | Everything meets the column. | One element crosses or bleeds past it. | When the content genuinely continues past the edge. Never for emphasis. | 1 / section |
| 04 | Two elevation levels. | One element casts a hard offset instead of a soft one. | For something temporarily on top of the work rather than part of it. | 1 / view |
| 05 | Plates carry no fixed meaning. | One element claims a plate semantically. | When the action is irreversible, and the label says so in words too. | 1 / flow |
| 06 | Nothing sits off-axis. | One element rotates, up to 1.5°. | Where the thing depicted is physically loose: a plate, a sticker, a slip of paper. | 1 / page |
| 07 | Density is uniform. | One section takes twice the air, or half. | Where a decision is asked for and the page should visibly slow down first. | 1 / page |

## Law 00 — two breaches in one view cancel each other

Not discouraged. **Cancelled.** Attention does not accumulate, it divides: at
one licensed violation the exception carries full weight; at two, each carries
half, which is worse than none, because the reader now has two candidates for
the important thing and no way to choose.

    w(v) = (1 / v) · w_max

A screen that needs two breaks has a hierarchy problem, not a styling one.

## A breach used twice is an undeclared token

The second time a violation appears, it stops being one. Name it, give it a
slot, write its states — or stop using it.

## The law must survive the breach

If breaking a law makes the law look arbitrary, the law was wrong. Delete it
rather than licensing around it.

## What each law costs when broken without a licence

01 reads as a bug · 02 reads as a mistake · 03 reads as broken layout ·
04 reads as a stray card · 05 teaches a false rule · 06 reads as CSS drift ·
07 reads as unfinished.
